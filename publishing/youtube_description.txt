How can a person inspect, and correct, the evidence a neural network uses? Concept bottleneck models predict through a layer of named, human-readable concepts that can be read, evaluated and edited. A graduate lecture film for ES 667 Deep Learning at IIT Gandhinagar. The slides are generated from the same source as this film, one slide step per narration line.

Covers: a real CLIP black box; post-hoc explanations (saliency, occlusion, and TCAV run on CUB) and their limits (sanity checks, shortcuts, pixels versus findings) versus interpretable-by-design models; the concept bottleneck x → ĉ → ŷ; the CUB-200-2011 benchmark; how a concept becomes a number; worked arithmetic of the task head; independent, sequential and joint training, measured on CUB; human interventions and when a correct edit hurts; incomplete vocabularies and concept leakage; the supervision ladder from labelled concepts to training-free models, with worked vectors for concept embedding models, post-hoc CBMs, label-free CBMs, LaBo, VLG-CBM and classification by description; evaluation; and concept bottlenecks beyond images.
