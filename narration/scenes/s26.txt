## s26 | Appendix: testing with concept activation vectors | 0.4 | 0.8
[[TCAV asks about a named {c:concept}: does the model use {c:grey wings} when it says {y:herring gull}?]] This appendix adds one more post-hoc method, one that asks about concepts directly. Testing with concept activation vectors, or TCAV, asks a question like this one: does the model use grey wings when it says herring gull? Here the model is CLIP, the black box from Part 1. || 0.8
[[Step 1: collect photos {c:with grey wings} and photos {m:without}]] {tags: [calm]} The first step is to collect example photos: some birds with grey wings, and some without, here from a labelled bird dataset. None of them is a herring gull. || 0.6
[[Step 2: the {c:concept direction} separates them inside the model]] Pass every photo through the model and look at its internal activations. Fit a line that separates the two groups. The direction across that line is the concept activation vector: moving along it means more grey wing. || 0.8
[[Step 3: nudge each {y:herring gull} photo along that direction]] Now take a herring gull photo and ask: if its activation moved a little in the grey wing direction, would the herring gull score go up? The gradient of the score, times the direction, gives the answer as a sign. || 0.8
[[The TCAV score: the share of photos whose {y:score} rises]] The TCAV score is the share of herring gull photos where the score goes up. For grey wings, it went up for almost all 60 photos, in each of 10 repeats with different example photos. || 0.8
[[Compare with random directions]] A direction that separates two random sets of photos gives scores that jump between zero and one. So a concept counts only when its score is steady and different from random ones. And even then, TCAV says what the model responds to. It gives no way to correct it. || 1.0
