## s19 | Comparing the variants | 0.4 | 0.8
[[One table: one row per variant, one column at a time]] Six variants are a lot to hold in mind at once. This table puts them beside the original CBM, one row per method, and we fill it one column at a time. || 0.8
[[Column one: the {y:supervision} each variant needs]] The first column is the data needed for training. Here x is an image, c its concept labels, and y its class label. Supervised CBMs and concept embedding models need all three. || 0.8
[[Most variants drop the {c:concept labels}]] Post-hoc models, label-free models, LaBo and VLG-CBM drop the concept labels. Classification by description, CBD in the table, needs no training data at all. || 0.8
[[Column two: where the {c:concepts} come from]] The second column says who supplies the concepts. For CBMs and concept embedding models, people do, through their labels. The others take them from a concept bank, a language model, CLIP, or a detector. || 0.8
[[Column three: what the {f:task head} reads]] The third column is what the task head actually reads. A CBM passes one number per concept, a concept embedding model a vector per concept, and a post-hoc model its projection scores plus a residual. Label-free models and VLG-CBM pass the outputs of a learned concept layer, and LaBo and classification by description pass raw CLIP scores. || 0.8
[[Column four: what is trained]] The last column is what gets trained. CBMs and concept embedding models train the concept predictor and the head. Post-hoc models and LaBo train only the head. Label-free models and VLG-CBM train a concept layer and the head. Classification by description trains nothing at all. || 1.0
[[Naming a {c:concept} does not ground it]] Each change makes concepts cheaper or predictions more accurate. None makes grounding automatic. A phrase from a language model, or a CLIP similarity, does not show that the model sees that property in the image. That has to be checked. || 1.2
[[Who supplies the {c:concepts}, and who checks them?]] One question sums up the table: who supplies the concepts, and who checks them? In CBMs and concept embedding models, people label the concepts, so people check them. In post-hoc models, a concept bank supplies them, checked only by whoever built it. Label-free models and LaBo take them from a language model, and only a CLIP similarity checks them. VLG-CBM keeps the language model, and adds a detector as the check. In classification by description, nobody checks. || 1.2
[[Each rung up the ladder removes a cost and a {e:check}]] This is the general trade-off. Each rung up the ladder removes a human labour cost, and with it a human check. The labour saved on labelling has to be spent later, on checking that the concepts are really in the image. || 1.2
