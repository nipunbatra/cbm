[[Labelling every {c:concept} on every {x:image} is the main cost]] The original concept bottleneck needs a person to label every concept on every training image. That is its main cost, and most newer methods try to reduce it. || 0.8
[[One training record: an {x:image}, its {c:concept labels} and a {y:class}]] One supervised training record looks like this: the image, a 0 or 1 for each named concept, and the class. On CUB, that meant 112 concept labels per photograph. || 0.8
[[Rung one: {x:images}, {c:concept labels} and {y:classes}]] The newer methods form a ladder of shrinking supervision. On the first rung, CBMs and concept embedding models need the image, the concept labels and the class. || 0.8
[[Rung two: drop the {c:concept labels}]] Post-hoc models, label-free models, LaBo and VLG-CBM drop the concept labels. || 0.8
[[Rung three: drop training altogether]] Classification by description drops training altogether. || 0.8
[[Who supplies the {c:concepts}? On rung one, a tutor marks every picture]] An everyday way to read the ladder is to ask who supplies the concepts. Think of learning to recognise animals. On rung one, a tutor marks every feature on every picture. || 0.6
[[Then a {x:spotter} with a field guide, then the field guide alone]] On rung two, an experienced spotter, a pretrained model, does the looking, and a field guide, a language model or a list of phrases, supplies the names.
