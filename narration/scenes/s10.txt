[[Labelling every {c:concept} on every {x:image} is the main cost]] The original concept bottleneck needs a person to label every concept on every training image. That is its main cost, and most newer methods try to reduce it. || 0.8
[[One training record: an {x:image}, its {c:concept labels} and a {y:class}]] One supervised training record looks like this: the image, a 0 or 1 for each named concept, and the class. On CUB, that meant 112 concept labels per photograph. || 0.8
[[Rung one: {x:images}, {c:concept labels} and {y:classes}]] The newer methods form a ladder of shrinking supervision. On the first rung, CBMs and concept embedding models need the image, the concept labels and the class. || 0.8
[[Rung two: keep {y:class labels}, drop per-image {c:concept labels}]] Post-hoc models, Language in a Bottle, or LaBo, and vision-language-guided concept bottleneck models, or VLG-CBMs, drop the concept labels on the task images. They still learn from images with class labels. A pretrained model supplies visual knowledge, and concept names can come from people or a language model. || 0.8
[[Rung three: no training for this task]] Classification by description drops training on the new task. It still uses a pretrained model. We supply class descriptions and a new image, without fitting a model to labelled examples of these classes. || 0.8
[[Rung two: {y:class labels} teach the {f:task head}]] Take Language in a Bottle, or LaBo, as an example of rung two. A language model supplies descriptions, such as black and white stripes. Pretrained CLIP compares each image with these descriptions and produces match scores. During training, class labels teach a task head how to combine those scores. For a new photo, the learned head predicts the class. We need class-labelled images, but no person has to label every concept in every image. The diagram illustrates the path, without showing measured scores. || 0.8
[[Rung three: combine the {c:match scores} with a fixed rule]] Now keep the pretrained CLIP model, but replace the learned head with a fixed rule. A language model supplies descriptions for each class. CLIP compares a new photo with each description. We average the match scores within each class and choose the class with the highest average. No class-labelled examples or fitting are needed for this task. The difference is the combination: rung two learns it from class labels; rung three uses a fixed average. || 0.8
[[Fewer {c:concept labels}, so check the {c:concepts} another way]] Dropping per-image concept labels saves labelling, but removes a direct check. A person who supplies a concept label has checked it against the image. On the higher rungs, a match score alone does not establish that the named concept is visible. We need to check this another way. || 0.6
