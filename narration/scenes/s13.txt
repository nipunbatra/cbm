[[Follow the path from {x:photo} to {c:concepts} to {y:answer}: what can fail?]] Readable concepts in the middle do not guarantee a good model. Let us follow the path from the photo x, through the concept scores c hat, to the answer y hat, and ask what can fail at each of five points. || 0.8
[[1 · {x:Encoder}: distribution shift]] First, distribution shift: the encoder meets data unlike its training data. In symbols, new inputs come from a different distribution than the training inputs. Recall the chest X-ray model from Part 1. || 0.4
[[A new hospital: the same model scores lower]] Its AUC, a score where 1 means perfect ranking and 0.5 means guessing, was about 0.93 on X-rays from the hospitals it learned from, and about 0.82 at a new hospital. In practice, the model looks fine where it was built and does worse at the new site. || 0.6
[[2 · {x:Concept detector}: wrong grounding]] Second, wrong grounding: the concept detector responds to the wrong evidence, such as the water behind a gull instead of its wingtip. In practice, the score looks right for the wrong reason, until a gull turns up away from water. || 0.6
[[3 · {c:Vocabulary}: missing information]] Third, missing information: the vocabulary leaves out what the task needs. In Part 8, a blue circle and a blue square shared one concept vector, so the best any head could do was guess, right half the time. In practice, two cases an expert would tell apart look identical to the head. || 0.6
[[4 · {f:Task head}: the wrong relationship]] Fourth, the task head can learn the wrong relationship. Recall the billing head from Part 7: it takes away 2 for each unit of duplicate charge, the wrong sign. A correct edit sets duplicate charge to 1, and billing falls from 0.80 to 0.45. In practice, the expert's fix is right, yet the decision moves the wrong way. || 0.6
[[5 · {e:Intervention}: wrong, or too costly]] Fifth, the intervention can be wrong, or too costly. On this chart from Part 11, each step to the right is one more correction per image, and each correction takes an expert's time. Ours came from true labels, an expert who never errs. In practice, a busy expert can misjudge a concept too, and every check uses time they could spend elsewhere. || 1.0
[[Five questions for any {c:concept bottleneck}]] So ask five questions of any concept bottleneck. Are the concepts meaningful? Are they grounded correctly? Are they sufficient for the task? Does the model actually use them? And do corrections improve decisions? || 1.2
